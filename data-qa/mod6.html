Module 6: Metabolism — Part 2 — Study Guide Q&A

Study Guide Questions

  1. What is aerobic metabolism (aka aerobic cellular respiration)?
    •  The breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen; released energy is used to make ATP.

  2. What is the overall chemical equation for aerobic cellular respiration?
    •  C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + Energy → 38 ADP + 38 P → 38 ATP.

  3. How many ATP are produced by bacteria during aerobic cellular respiration?
    •  38 ATP total.

  4. What occurs during glycolysis? How many ATP are produced?
    •  Glucose is split into 2 pyruvic acid molecules.
    •  Produces 2 ATP (net) and 2 NADH + 2 H+.

  5. What happens to hydrogen during glycolysis?
    •  Hydrogen (protons/electrons) released is picked up by the electron carrier NAD+, forming NADH, to be delivered later to the electron transport chain.

  6. What are electron carriers? Give two examples.
    •  Molecules that pick up and transport high-energy electrons (with hydrogen) between metabolic stages, ultimately to the electron transport chain.
    •  Examples: NAD+/NADH and FAD/FADH2.

  7. What occurs during the transition reaction (pyruvate oxidation)?
    •  Each of the 2 pyruvic acid molecules is converted into Acetyl-CoA, releasing 2 CO2 and producing 2 NADH + 2 H+.

  8. What occurs during the Krebs cycle? How many ATP are produced?
    •  The 2 Acetyl-CoA molecules are fully broken down, releasing 4 CO2.
    •  Produces 2 ATP, 6 NADH (+6 H+), and 2 FADH2.

  9. What is the electron transport chain, and where is it located in bacteria?
    •  A series of membrane proteins that pass electrons (from NADH/FADH2) down a chain, pumping H+ to build a proton gradient used to make ATP.
    •  Located in the cell (plasma) membrane in bacteria.

  10. What role does oxygen serve in the electron transport chain?
    •  Acts as the final electron acceptor — accepting electrons (and H+) at the end of the chain to form H2O.

  11. How does the electron transport chain produce ATP? How many ATP?
    •  Electrons moving down the chain power proton pumps that move H+ across the membrane, building a proton gradient; H+ then flows back through ATP synthase, driving ATP production.
    •  34 ATP.

  12. What is ATP synthase? How does it work?
    •  A membrane enzyme that uses the flow of H+ down its gradient (through the protein) to power synthesis of ATP from ADP + phosphate (chemiosmosis).

  13. What roles do active transport and facilitated diffusion play in the electron transport chain?
    •  Active transport (proton pumps) uses electron-transport energy to pump H+ against its gradient, building the proton gradient.
    •  Facilitated diffusion lets H+ flow back down its gradient through the ATP synthase channel, powering ATP production.

  14. What is anaerobic metabolism, and what is an example?
    •  The breakdown of glucose without oxygen.
    •  Example: fermentation (e.g., lactic acid fermentation by Lactobacillus, or alcoholic fermentation by Saccharomyces cerevisiae).

  15. How many ATP are made during fermentation?
    •  2 ATP (from glycolysis only).

  16. What are the two main types of fermentation (according to products)?
    •  Acidic (lactic acid) fermentation — produces lactic acid (bacteria and animals).
    •  Alcoholic fermentation — produces ethanol and CO2 (fungi/yeast).

  17. How do aerobic cellular respiration and fermentation differ?
    •  Aerobic respiration uses oxygen as the final electron acceptor, runs all four stages (glycolysis, transition, Krebs cycle, ETC), and yields 38 ATP.
    •  Fermentation uses no oxygen, stops after glycolysis, regenerates NAD+ by reducing pyruvate into lactic acid or ethanol/CO2, and yields only 2 ATP.

  18. What are oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions, and how do they relate to cellular respiration and fermentation?
    •  Reactions where electrons transfer between molecules: oxidation = losing electrons; reduction = gaining electrons.
    •  Glucose is oxidized (loses electrons/hydrogens) across these pathways; electron carriers (NAD+, FAD) or pyruvate are reduced (gain electrons/hydrogens) as energy is extracted step by step.

Additional Key Terms (from key-terms list, not covered above)

  • Metabolic Pathway — a series of connected chemical reactions in a cell (e.g., glycolysis, Krebs cycle).
  • Cations — positively charged ions (e.g., H+), relevant to the proton gradient discussed above.
  • Oxidative Phosphorylation — the overall process of using the electron transport chain and ATP synthase (powered by oxidation reactions) to generate ATP.
  • Pyruvate Oxidation and Pyruvate Reduction — pyruvate is oxidized (loses electrons) during the transition reaction in aerobic respiration; pyruvate is reduced (gains electrons) during fermentation to form lactic acid or ethanol.
