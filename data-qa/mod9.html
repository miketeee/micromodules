Module 9: Eukaryotes — Protists — Study Guide Q&A

Study Guide Questions

  1. What is the endosymbiotic theory and what evidence supports it?
    •  The theory that eukaryotic cells arose when a larger prokaryotic cell engulfed smaller bacterial cells, which began living and reproducing inside it instead of being destroyed, eventually becoming organelles — ingested photosynthetic bacteria became chloroplasts; ingested aerobic bacteria became mitochondria.
    •  Evidence: mitochondria/chloroplasts have a bacteria-like rod shape (1–10 µm), a double membrane, prokaryotic ribosomes, a circular chromosome, replicate by binary fission, and show DNA sequence homology to bacteria.

  2. Who proposed the endosymbiotic theory?
    •  Lynn Margulis (1938–2011). Her paper, "On the Origin of Mitosing Cells," was rejected by around 15 journals before publication, and both she and the theory faced sustained criticism for decades before it became accepted.

  3. What are some distinguishing characteristics of protists?
    •  A diverse group of unicellular and multicellular eukaryotes — essentially a "miscellaneous" kingdom.
    •  Split into Protozoa (heterotrophs) and Algae (autotrophs).

  4. What are protozoans, and what are some of their mechanisms of motility?
    •  Unicellular, heterotrophic protists.
    •  Move via cilia, flagella, or pseudopods, depending on the subgroup.

  5. What are trophozoites?
    •  The active, feeding stage of a protozoan.

  6. What are cysts?
    •  A dormant stage of a protozoan, often the stage that gets transmitted between hosts.

  7. Major subgroups of protozoans:

Euglenozoans Euglena, Trypanosoma Flagella (each containing a supportive rod) —

  1. How does metronidazole (Flagyl) work?
    •  It blocks DNA synthesis in protozoans.

  2. What are algae?
    •  Unicellular or multicellular, autotrophic (photosynthetic) protists; mostly harmless. Examples: dinoflagellates and marine diatoms.

  3. What is a red tide? Organisms involved? Why are red tides important?
    •  A bloom of a particular dinoflagellate that produces a neurotoxin, turning coastal water red.
    •  The toxin paralyzes and suffocates fish, causing mass die-offs; shellfish can also accumulate the toxin, sickening people who eat contaminated shellfish.

  4. What are diatoms? Why are diatoms important (their uses)?
    •  Marine algae with a two-piece cell wall made of glass (silica).
    •  Important because: their glass shells are inert and don't break down, accumulating on ocean floors as diatomaceous earth — used in gardening (aerates soil, retains water, and acts as a natural insecticide since sharp glass fragments damage insects that ingest it), in reflective paint, and in tooth polish. Diatoms are also major photosynthetic oxygen producers.

Microbe Spotlight - Giardia — Archaezoan; 2 nuclei, 8 flagella; trophozoite and cyst stages; causes fatty diarrhea (Giardiasis). - Trichomonas — Archaezoan; single nucleus; trophozoite stage only; an STD; causes vaginitis. - Euglena — Euglenozoan; both heterotrophic and autotrophic; harmless. - Trypanosoma — Euglenozoan; blood-borne pathogen; causes sleeping sickness (Trypanosomiasis). - Amoeba — Amoebozoan; harmless. - Entamoeba (E. histolytica) — Amoebozoan; causes amoebic dysentery (bloody diarrhea). - Plasmodium — Apicomplexan; causes malaria; infects ~10% of the world's population (300–500 million new cases/year); transmitted by the Anopheles mosquito; lysing of red blood cells releases waste causing fever and chills. - Cryptosporidium — Apicomplexan; primary cause of recreational diarrhea; lives inside intestinal cells; resistant to chlorine.

Disease Spotlight - Amoebic Dysentery (Entamoeba), Fatty Diarrhea/Giardiasis (Giardia), Malaria (Plasmodium), Recreational Diarrhea (Cryptosporidium), Sleeping Sickness/Trypanosomiasis (Trypanosoma), Vaginitis (Trichomonas).

Scientist Spotlight - Lynn Margulis — proposed the Endosymbiotic Theory. - Anton van Leeuwenhoek — see Module 1.

Antibiotic (Antiprotozoal) Spotlight - Metronidazole (Flagyl) — blocks DNA synthesis. - Chloroquine — prevents Plasmodium trophozoites from degrading hemoglobin.
